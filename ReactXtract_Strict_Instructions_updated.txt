## Mandatory Reaction Evidence Gate

Create a reaction record only when the supplied source supports an actual preparative chemical transformation. At least one of the following must be present:

- An explicit statement that a product, intermediate, or compound was prepared, formed, obtained, afforded, synthesized, or isolated.
- A reaction scheme or table entry with identifiable starting material or reactant, a transformation arrow, and an identifiable product or product label.
- An experimental procedure that connects specified starting material(s) to a specified product or source compound label.
- An explicitly successful product-forming scope or optimization entry.

Do not create a reaction record solely because a chemical name, compound label, analytical result, or reaction-related word appears on the page.

Do not extract the following as reactions:

- Proposed mechanisms or mechanistic intermediates that were not experimentally prepared.
- Retrosynthetic arrows or synthetic planning diagrams.
- Biological assays, docking studies, molecular-dynamics studies, or formulation steps.
- Characterization-only entries, spectra, melting points, NMR, IR, HRMS, MS, or elemental-analysis data without a supported preparation.
- Work-up, quenching, extraction, washing, drying, filtration, evaporation, chromatography, recrystallization, or storage operations by themselves.
- Failed, trace, zero-yield, or explicitly "no reaction" experiments.
- A general reaction family inferred from chemistry knowledge when the source does not report a specific transformation.

If the evidence is insufficient to prove that a preparative transformation occurred, do not create a reaction record.

## Distinct-Reaction Rules

- Create one record for each distinct product-forming transformation.
- Keep separately labeled products, substrate-scope examples, intermediates, and explicitly successful optimization entries separate when the source provides reaction-specific support.
- When multiple products were explicitly prepared by the same general procedure, create one record per product and use shared conditions only when the source explicitly links that product or series to the procedure.
- When a single arrow contains sequential transformations without an isolated intermediate, keep one reaction record and report the supported number of stages.
- When an intermediate is explicitly prepared or isolated before a later transformation, create separate records for the intermediate-forming and subsequent product-forming reactions.
- Do not duplicate the same reaction because it appears in the abstract, scheme, results, experimental section, and supporting information. Merge only descriptions that clearly refer to the same transformation and product.
- Preserve separate alternative methods, conditions, or experiments when the source treats them as distinct reactions.

## Field Classification Rules

### 1. Products

- Include only substances explicitly formed, obtained, afforded, synthesized, isolated, or shown at the product end of the applicable reaction arrow.
- Products of the reaction can be specified together with the yield of each product and with respective information about enantiomeric excess or diastereomeric excess
- Compounds with yield/excess >5% are acceptable as product.
- Compounds with yield/excess equal or less than 5%, if separated, with fact, or are used as starting materials are acceptable as product.
- Prefer the exact reported chemical name. If no name is reported, use the exact source label, such as "compound 4a" or "intermediate L-6".
- Do not invent a chemical name by interpreting a drawn structure.
- Do not classify a compound as a product merely because it was characterized, tested, discussed, or used in a later step.

### 2. Starting Materials

- Include only reactants present before the transformation that are explicitly consumed or incorporated into the reported product.
- Only compounds that contribute carbon atoms to products are considered as starting materials. 
- Compounds which do not contribute carbon atoms to products are considered as catalysts or reagents
- Use the source name or compound label exactly as reported.
- Do not infer incorporation from general chemical knowledge alone.
- Do not include catalysts, solvents, reagents, quenching agents, or work-up materials as starting materials.

### 3. Reagents

- Compounds which are simultaneously acting as reagents or catalysts and starting materials in a reaction must be entered as starting materials only.
- Compounds which are simultaneously acting as reagents or catalysts and solvents in a reaction must be entered as either a reagent or catalyst only.
- Include substances used to cause, promote, activate, reduce, oxidize, protect, deprotect, couple, or otherwise enable the transformation.
- Exclude starting materials, products, catalysts, and reaction solvents unless the source explicitly supports a genuine dual role.
- Exclude substances used only for quenching, extraction, washing, drying, chromatography, recrystallization, or other work-up operations.

### 4. Catalysts

- Compounds which are simultaneously acting as reagents or catalysts and starting materials in a reaction must be entered as starting materials only.
- Compounds which are simultaneously acting as reagents or catalysts and solvents in a reaction must be entered as either a reagent or catalyst only.
- Include a substance only when the source identifies it as a catalyst or explicitly supports catalytic use or loading.
- Preserve reported catalyst loading, such as mol%, under Other reaction conditions when available.
- Do not infer catalysis from chemical plausibility, common practice, or a small quantity alone.
- Do not duplicate the catalyst under Reagents unless the source explicitly describes a dual role.
- Compounds added as internal standards or indicators are not considered catalysts and must be ignored.


### 5. Solvents

- Compounds which are simultaneously acting as reagents or catalysts and solvents in a reaction must be entered as either a reagent or catalyst only.
- Solvents used in the respective reaction stage must be captured
- Solvents must be selected from the lookup list. If the used solvent is not available, the entry must be omitted.
- Solvents used in one stage must be re-entered in the following stages if they were not removed.
- Aq. buffer, aq. acetate buffer and aq. phosphate buffer are considered as solvents.
- Include only media used during the chemical transformation.
- In cases where author used hexanes, butanol, dioxane, etc. use hexane, 1-butanol, 1,4-dioxane, respectively from the lookup list.
- For aqueous solutions such 1N HCl or 20% HCl and analogues, enter as HCl and water.
- Preserve mixtures and ratios exactly as reported.
- Exclude liquids used solely for quenching, dilution after completion, extraction, washing, chromatography, recrystallization, or analytical measurement.
- When the same solvent is used during both reaction and work-up, include it only because its reaction-medium use is explicitly supported.
- Compounds added as internal standards or indicators are not considered reagents and must be ignored.

### 6. Reaction Time

- Report only the duration of the chemical transformation.
- Preserve the exact value and original units.
- For supported multistage reactions, preserve stage-specific times in their reported sequence.
- Do not calculate or sum a total time unless the source explicitly reports that total.
- Exclude work-up, extraction, drying, purification, crystallization, storage, and analytical times.

### 7. Reaction Pressure

- Report pressure only when explicitly stated for the reaction, such as atm, bar, psi, kPa, MPa, vacuum, autogenous pressure, or a clearly defined sealed-pressure condition.
- Reaction pressure (range) is captured at which the reaction stage was carried out.
- Report pressure in ranges when the lowest and highest reaction values are provided. 
- Enter atmospheric pressure as 1 atm 
- Preserve the exact value and units.
- Do not convert nitrogen, argon, oxygen, air, or another atmosphere into a pressure unless an actual pressure is stated.
- Do not assume that reflux, a sealed tube, or reduced-pressure evaporation defines the reaction pressure.

### 8. Reaction Temperature

- Reaction temperature (range) is captured at which the reaction stage was carried out.
- Report temperature in ranges when the lowest and highest reaction values are provided. 
- Preserve the exact value and units.
- Ambient temperature and room temperature must be converted to 20°C.
- Addition of reaction participants or components just before heating or reflux are not captured.
- The cooling or warming to room temperature just before work-up are not captured.

### 9. Other Reaction Conditions

- Include only conditions that apply to the chemical transformation: temperature, atmosphere, concentration, equivalents, stoichiometry, catalyst loading, order or rate of addition, irradiation, wavelength, microwave conditions, electrochemical conditions, current, voltage, pH, and other explicitly reported reaction parameters.
- Preserve the source wording, values, sequence, and units.
- Keep stage-specific conditions associated with the correct stage.
- Exclude work-up, extraction, washing, drying, filtration, evaporation, chromatography, recrystallization, and characterization conditions.
- Redundant information must be avoided.
- For a standard hydrogenation, the keyword "inert atmosphere" must not be entered.
- Information about molecular sieve must always be captured as "Molecular sieve" in other reaction conditions field

### 10. Number of Reaction Stages

- Single-stage reaction is a chemical reaction where no intermediates have been detected or postulated.
- Count a one-pot sequence as multiple stages only when known or unknown intermediates are detected or postulated.
- In multi-stage reactions, at least one starting material must be present in stage 1.
- Generation of reagents or catalysts in situ is not considered a reaction stage.
- Count distinct sequential chemical transformations represented by the reaction record.
- Do not count reagent additions, temperature changes, stirring periods, sampling, quenching, extraction, washing, filtration, drying, evaporation, chromatography, or recrystallization as reaction stages.
- Do not infer a stage count when the source does not support it.

### 11. Type of Reaction Description

- Indicate where the chemical reaction information was captured
- Limit choices to Experimental part, Scheme or table, and Body text
- In case that more than one of the above mentioned keywords maybe applicable, choose only one based on the following priorities :Experimental part > Scheme or table > Body text

### 12. Yield

- Yields are entered if the compound is isolated and with purity of at least 95%; ee/de equal or greater than 90%, except yields such as %Chromat. and %Spectr.
- Crude yields without any specification of purity must be ignored.
- Yields must not be calculated.
- When yields are given in ranges, the highest value is taken.
- Yields with above 100% must be omitted.
- Yields given for a specific product must be entered as Yield(%)
- Yields given in other units, not as % values, must be captured and entered as Yield(other)
- If more than one product is given and the sum of the yields of all entered products are given in %, capture it as Overall yield (%)
- If more than one product is given and the sum of the yields of all entered products are given in other units, not as % values, capture it as Overall yield (other)

## Evidence and Confidence Requirements

For every field, provide field metadata containing:

- confidence from 0 to 1;
- source filename;
- original source page number;
- a short exact supporting quote; and
- a normalized bounding box with left, top, right, and bottom coordinates from 0 to 1 when visually available.

Apply these rules:

- The evidence must support both the field value and its assigned role in this specific reaction.
- A chemical name appearing somewhere on the page is not sufficient evidence that it is a product, starting material, reagent, catalyst, or reaction solvent.
- Prefer evidence from the experimental procedure, applicable scheme or table entry, or an explicit statement connecting the chemical to the transformation.
- General-procedure evidence may support a specific example only when the source explicitly links that example to the procedure.
- Confidence measures source support and classification certainty, not chemical plausibility.
- Use confidence 0 when a field is empty.
- Never increase confidence because a value is chemically expected or common.
- Do not invent a quote, filename, page, bounding box, condition, quantity, identity, or role.

## Missing Values

- Return an empty array for an unavailable list field: Products, Starting materials, Reagents, Catalysts, or Solvents.
- Return an empty string for an unavailable scalar field: Reaction time, Reaction pressure, Other reaction conditions, or Number of reaction stages.
- For an unavailable field, use confidence 0, empty source filename, empty page, empty quote, and a zero bounding box.
- Do not use placeholders such as "Not reported", "Unknown", "N/A", or guessed values.
- Do not omit an otherwise valid reaction merely because one or more fields are unavailable.

## Chemical Text Fidelity

- Preserve chemical names, stereochemistry, compound labels, quantities, and units as reported.
- Use Unicode subscript digits for molecular-formula indices, for example H₂O, Na₂SO₄, CH₂Cl₂, and K₂CO₃.
- Use Unicode superscripts for explicitly shown charges or isotopes when supported by the source.
- Do not turn locants, compound labels, quantities, times, pages, or temperatures into subscripts. Keep forms such as 1,2-dichloro, compound 3a, 20 mL, 24 h, page 12, and 80 °C unchanged.
